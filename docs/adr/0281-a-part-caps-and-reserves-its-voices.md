# 0281. Give each Part a MAX VOICES cap and a RESERVE floor in the shared pool

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Deciders:** owner (approved #966 on 2026-11-15, from dspmax's request in chimera-releases#5), firmware

## Context
Chimera's six Parts share one pool of 8 voices (ADR 0015, 0027, 0040). When the pool is full, rules 3 and 4 of `Allocator::pick_where` steal the oldest non-mono voice of any Part. The load net's `shed_one` (ADR 0273) also picks across all Parts. Stock PreenFM3 gives each timbre a fixed set of voices (`Synth.cpp:719-736, 776-790`) and steals only within the timbre (`Timbre.cpp:378-433`). In chimera-releases#5, a VA pad with long held notes cut out whenever Part 5 got busy. MONO on Part 5 was too little. The owner approved a per-Part cap and floor (#966). The default had to keep today's behaviour bit for bit.

## Decision
- **The types** (`chimera_engine::voice_limit`). Illegal states can't be built, and the rule lives in one place:
  - `VoiceCount` is 0..=`POOL` (8, `MAX_VOICES`), and `VoiceCount::VALUES` (9) is each cell's list length. `VoiceCap` is 1..=8. `MaxVoices` is `Auto` or `Cap(VoiceCap)`. `Reserve` wraps a private `VoiceCount`. `Edge` names a list's end.
  - Each cell steps in its own type, saturating: `MaxVoices::stepped(delta)` and `ended(Edge)`, `Reserve::stepped` and `ended`. A stored value converts once, saturating, through `MaxVoices::from_f32` and `Reserve::from_f32` (NaN and below 0 are 0). `MaxVoices::at(VoiceCount)` is total, so no conversion has a silent default arm.
  - `VoiceLimit::new(max, reserve) -> Result<VoiceLimit, OverCap>` builds a pair whose reserve is within its cap by construction. Its fields are private.
  - `Booked::new(sum) -> Option<Booked>` holds a sum within the pool. `Booked::sum(reserves) -> Result<Booked, Overbooked>` adds reserves, typed. `Overbooked` is a sum past the pool.
  - `Limits` is a lawful set: private fields, each Part's `VoiceLimit` and their `Booked`, changed only through `try_set(PartId, VoiceLimit) -> Result<(), Refusal>`.
  - `Asked { max, reserve }` is a Part's two cells as set or saved, lawful or not, and is what the Part block holds. `over_cap()` is by how much its reserve passes its cap (L1's method).
  - `Over` is a set saved past the rule. `Shares::of([Asked; 6])` makes `Lawful(Limits)` when every pair is lawful and the reserves fit, else `Over(Over)`: an old or hand-edited file loads as saved, as this explicit second type.
  - `Shares::refusal(PartId, Asked) -> Option<Refusal>` is the one rule (`Refusal::{OverCap, Overbooked}`). On a lawful set it refuses anything past the rule. On an `Over` set it refuses an edit that raises either excess (reserve over cap, or reserves over the pool), so a project saved past the rule can only come down, as an old kit does in ADR 0280. The UI's turn and edit, any `edit_in` path (a CC, the console), and the allocator all read the same `Shares`, built by one constructor, `Shares::from_parts`, from each Part's mix in Part order.
- **MAX VOICES (the cap).** A Part's live voices never exceed its cap. Rule 1 (Mono retrigger) and rule 2 (a key re-struck) come first, as before, because they reuse a voice the Part already owns. A new note on a Part at its cap does not take a free voice. It steals one of the Part's own voices, using rule 3's order (oldest tail, then oldest held note), even with free voices in the pool. If that steal does not fit the CPU budget, the note is refused (rule 4). AUTO caps nothing.
  - **A capped Part always reuses its own voice.** Normally that is a non-mono voice. When the Part's only live voice is a mono one, left over after MODE was switched MONO→POLY while it rang, a Poly note at the cap takes that mono voice instead of being refused. Another Part's mono voice is still never stolen (ADR 0015).
- **RESERVE (the floor) guards what still plays a key or a hit** (the owner's rulings, 2026-11-18). A Part is guarded while its reserving voices number no more than its reserve. A note's voice reserves while its key is down; a DRUM hit's reserves until its voice finishes, its pad's note-off or not. The distinction is a type: a booked slot's `Strike` is `Note(Key)` or `Hit(Key)` (`Key::{Down, Up}`), a free slot has none (`Option<Strike>`), `Allocator::book` books a note and `book_hit` a DRUM hit, and `Strike::reserves` is `Note(Down)` or any `Hit`. A hit's slot frees when its voice finishes, whatever its key (`release_finished` frees any `Hit`): a hit whose pad is held down, or whose source sends no note-off, never holds a slot or a guard past its sound, and its late note-off finds nothing held. Another Part's note can't steal a guarded Part's reserving voices (rules 3 and 4). A released note's tail is fair game and doesn't count toward the reserve. A Part's own note may still take its own voices (at its cap, or in rule 3 when the oldest voice is its own). A Part with more reserving voices than its reserve can lose them one at a time, down to its reserve. A reserve doesn't hold free voices back: a Part with nothing sounding still gets a free voice first, or steals an unguarded voice. A reserve of 0 guards nothing. Mono voices are never stolen (ADR 0015), guarded or not.
  - The cap still counts every live voice, tails included: a tail still sounds and still costs its slot, and at the cap the Part's own oldest tail is what its next note takes first.
- **What a note may steal** is one set, `Allocator::stealable(PartId) -> VoiceSet`. At the cap it is the Part's own live voices. Otherwise it is every voice but another Part's guarded ones (`card_stealable`). Rules 3 and 4 take the oldest within `stealable ∩ Steal::pool()` (ADR 0280's DRUM mask).
- **TAPE's card steals keep off guarded voices.** A streamed note's admission can stop another voice's stream to free card bandwidth (`AdmitDoor`, ADR 0175, 0183). `stream::admit::admit` takes a `stealable: VoiceSet`, and only a voice in it may be stopped. Both card-steal paths, `admit_stream` (a streamed note-on, `Admit::Steal`) and `admit_mid` (a re-bill mid-note), pass `Allocator::card_stealable`: every voice but another Part's guarded ones, at the cap too. The cap limits only how many voices the Part sounds; bandwidth isn't a voice (the owner's ruling, 2026-11-18). No card steal takes a guarded Part's reserving voice. With nothing stealable that frees room, the note is refused, as rule 4 refuses. `tape_stream_test` pins both: a guarded held stream is kept while RESERVE 0 lets it go, and a MONO Part at cap 1 steals card room as on AUTO.
- **Shed (ADR 0027, 0273).** `shed_one` keeps its order (non-mono first, the oldest tail, then the newest held note), but guarded voices go last: `(guarded, mono, held, age)`. A budget shed or the load net takes an unguarded voice whenever one sounds. It takes a guarded voice only when every voice left is guarded. The reserve protects a Part from other Parts' notes. An overrun belongs to no Part, and a block past its deadline glitches every Part, so the net must always be able to free a voice. The cap needs nothing in shed: shedding only lowers a count.
- **Defaults are today's, and that is tested against the old code.** With every Part on AUTO and 0, or every Part capped at the whole pool, the allocator makes the same choices as before. `chimera-engine/tests/voice_alloc_baseline_test.rs` proves it against `voice_alloc.rs` as it was at 33794f79, kept as a fixture (`tests/baseline/`). Both are driven through a seeded walk that checks every pick, `pick_hit`, shed, `shed_one`, the refused count, the cost and every slot, at every step. The walk covers mono and poly, four budgets, re-strike masks, `Any` and `Only`, release, kill, recost and `fade_from`: 2 × 199 seeds × 4 budgets × 3000 steps, about a second. The audio goldens are bit-identical.
- **The allocator is typed.** `VoiceSlot::part` is an `Option<PartId>`, and its hold is a `Strike`, not a bool. `pick`, `pick_hit` and `pick_where` take a `PartId` and return a `VoiceIdx`. `book`, `book_hit`, `release`, `renote`, `fade_from`, `recost`, `recost_with_glide`, `kill` and `release_finished` take a `VoiceIdx`, and `shed` returns one. The round-robin cursor is a `VoiceIdx` (`wrapping_add`), and the dying-voice pick keeps its `VoiceIdx`. Its own scans walk `VoiceIdx::ALL` through `slot(VoiceIdx)`. `live`, `reserving`, `capped` (an exhaustive match) and `guarded` read `Shares`. `VoiceSet::count` is `VoiceCount::of_pool_bits`, proven by a const assert that a `VoiceSet`'s `u8` has the pool's 8 bits. In the `Instrument`, `sounding` is `[Option<PartId>; 8]`, so a voice that never sounded claims no Part. Render skips such a voice but still runs its step 5 (`free_if_quiet`): a deferred note that is shed before it ever sounds (CC 120, the load net) frees its slot and its Sympathetic promise when nothing waits on it, as before. `pick_one` returns a `VoiceIdx`, and the voice loops walk `VoiceIdx::ALL`, indexing the voice arrays at the boundary. Its `part_id(q)` modulo is gone. `played::note_on` takes a `PartId`.
- **What can't be honoured is refused on the cell (ADR 0151's pattern).**
  - A Part's RESERVE may not exceed its own MAX VOICES: `RESERVE n · VOICES m`. Turning MAX VOICES below its RESERVE is refused with the same reason.
  - The six Parts' reserves may not sum past the pool's 8: `RESERVES n · POOL HOLDS 8`. A sum is a `Reserved` (0..=48), within the pool a `Booked` and past it an `Overbooked`. `Asked::over_cap` is a `VoiceCount`. `Reserved::of` and `Booked::sum` take one `Reserve` a Part (`&[Reserve; 6]`), so the sum's bound holds by construction; `Shares::from_parts` takes `&[Asked; 6]`. A reserve of 8 on one Part is allowed: that Part's voices can never be stolen by another Part's note.
  - An encoder turn onto a refused value lands on it dimmed, with the reason in the focus band, and nothing is written. The next turn continues from the held value. The hold is `held::Held::Voices(VoicesHold { to: VoiceTurn, seen: Seen })`. `VoiceTurn` is the cell's choice, `Max(MaxVoices)` or `Reserve(Reserve)`. `Seen` is what it was judged against: the Part's own pair and the other Parts' reserves summed. Like ADR 0280's held CHOKE, it carries no project revision. A CC on any Part, or any other edit, leaves it held. A change to what it was judged against (a load, another Part's RESERVE) drops it.
  - A set that no turn held (any `edit_in` path) is worked out on a copy and not written. Its reason is shown once as a toast.
- **The count is on the page.** The RESERVE cell's focus band shows `RESERVES n/8` in its hint's place, the sum over all six Parts, over 8 on a project saved past the rule.
- **Where the cells live.** MIXER › PRT is full (CH, MODE, OUT, LEVEL, PAN, BEND), so PRT gets one sub-page, VOICES (`VOI`, `MixPage::Voices`). It is down from PRT as a sub-page is (ADR 0081, 0145): EDIT under STACK, SEQ under ROW, and back up the other way. Until now EDIT on PRT went straight down to the Sound rung. Under STACK it now reaches VOICES first, and EDIT on VOICES goes on to the Sound rung. Under ROW, EDIT from PRT still goes to the Sound rung. Its nav step is a `MixStep::{ToPage(MixPage), ToSound, Pass}` worked out once, with an exhaustive match; VOICES is PART's sub-page `VOICES_SUB` (1), asserted against `PART_VOICES`. Its cells are MODE, MAX VOICES and RESERVE, so the cap sits beside MONO/POLY. MODE stays on PRT too. RESERVE fits a cell in full. MAX VOICES is 76 px against the cell's 62, so its cell reads `VOICES`, the full word that fits, and the refusal says `VOICES` to match. Its disk ident stays `MAX_VOICES`. The cells' name tables are tied to their types by a test: `names[code]` is `from_code(code)` as shown. No hidden gesture is added.
- **DRUM (ADR 0265, 0280).** The rules compose, because a DRUM Part's cap is its own smaller pool:
  - A hit's `Steal` (ADR 0280) is applied inside the Part's voices at the cap: `Steal::Only(own) ∩ the Part's live voices`, or every voice of the Part for `Any`. A STACK pad with live voices still ends only its own oldest voice. A CUT pad's hit, or a pad with no voice left, at the cap ends the Part's oldest voice: the oldest tail first, then the oldest held. That voice may be another pad's. ADR 0280 already lets such a hit steal any voice of the pool; the cap only narrows the pool to the Part.
  - Chokes and CUT run first (`Instrument::choke`). The voices they end are dying, so they no longer count toward the cap, and the hit takes a free or dying slot as before.
  - A kit holds at most 8 voice groups (ADR 0280). A DRUM Part capped below its group count sounds at most its cap at once, and the next group's hit takes the Part's oldest voice. This is the player's choice and is not refused on the cell. The `GROUPS n/8` note on CHOKE is unchanged.
  - A DRUM Part's RESERVE guards its hits from other Parts' notes and their card steals, until each hit's voice finishes, its pad's note-off or not. This is the use ADR 0280 deferred to #966: a busy synth Part can no longer take a hit's tail.
- **Mono.** A Mono Part retriggers its one voice (rule 1), which is never stolen. A cap of 1 counts voices the same as Mono, but it steals rather than retriggers, so a new note on a capped Poly Part starts a new voice in the Part's slot with its own envelope.
- **One byte with MODE (AXI).** Each Part's block is copied into the audio's three snapshots (ADR 0021). `PartParams` was 16 B with no padding, so any new field cost 4 B × 6 Parts × 3 = 72 B of AXI. That put ORBIT 12 B under ADR 0087's 32 KiB margin. So MODE, MAX VOICES and RESERVE share one byte, `part::Voicing`.
  - The byte is a mixed-radix number, `(mode × VALUES + max) × VALUES + reserve`, with radices `MODES` = 2 and `VALUES` = 9, named constants. Each digit is below its radix, so the byte is below `VOICINGS` = 2 × 9 × 9 = 162 (asserted to fit a `u8`).
  - `new(PartMode, Asked)` is the only way in. `MODES` is `PartMode::ALL.len()`, so a new mode can't slip past the radix. Every byte it makes decodes totally, because each digit taken out (`/ VALUES % VALUES`, `% VALUES`) is a count by construction. There is no silent default arm.
  - `PartParams` replaces its `mode` field with `voicing`, read and written through `mode()`, `set_mode`, `voices()` and `set_voices`. It stays 16 B (asserted in `part_voices_test`, with every combination round-tripped).
- **Saved with the Part.** The Part block holds the pair at rest, as `Asked`, lawful or not. It is written by `Block::write` (through `from_f32`) or by a disk code (through `from_code`). Every live edit is judged by `Shares::refusal` before it reaches the block. A load is lenient by design and classified on read (`Shares::of`): lawful, or `Over`. The pair is stored as `PartParams::MAX_VOICES` (13) and `RESERVE` (14), choice cells stored by enum code: MAX VOICES AUTO = 0 and 1–8, RESERVE 0–8. Disk idents are `AUTO`, `1`..`8` and `0`..`8`. An old project has neither id and loads with AUTO and 0, the target's values (the codec resets a Part's mix first). An unknown code keeps the default.
- **MIDI and `ed`.** Only LEVEL and PAN on the Part block are mappable (`midi_map::Mappable`, ADR 0217), so MAX VOICES and RESERVE get no CC or learn, the same as CH and MODE. The NRPN chart lists them as `R` (an address reserved, which writes nothing). `ed set` reaches only ALGO's cells (ADR 0276), so the console can't set them. Neither was added.
- **The audio side reads the settings once a snapshot.** `Instrument::recost` hands `Allocator::set_shares` the six Parts' pairs (`shares_of`, a rolled loop, out of line), before any note-on of the block is judged. There is no allocation and no lock. On AUTO, the per-pick cost is one match on the Part's `MaxVoices`. `live` is scanned only for a capped Part, and guard sets are built only where rule 3 steals or a shed picks, and only when some Part reserves a voice.
- **Bank placement (ADR 0198, 0251; bank 1 frozen).**
  - Bank 1 holds the allocator's cap and guard, `set_shares`, the card steal's `stealable` argument, the `voice_limit` types and rule, and the two specs with their get/write/code arms. The audio path can't call through a door, and specs are closed-door data. The PRT sub-page's `BlockDef`, its row word and its nav are bank-1 data too.
  - `ui::part_voices` holds the turn, the edit, the held check, the toast and the words. It links into bank 2's S4 group behind one `UiDoor` entry, `voices`. The entry takes an `Ask`: `Judge(Job::{Act, Held})`, `Why` or `Note`. Each ask carries its own typed answer slot: `Turned { Wrote, Unchanged, Held, Refused }`, `Result<(), Refusal>` or `Line`. Bank 1's callers never match a reply they don't expect.
  - Bank 1 keeps one out-of-line stub for both turns and edits (`UiState::voices_act`), and one each for the held check (`voices_held`) and the notes (`view::kit_or_voices_note`, one call for CHOKE's and the voice cells'). `ToastTimer::shows`' instance is globbed into the same group, since only bank-2 code calls it; so is `Shares::refusal`, should it ever get a symbol of its own (it is inlined into `part_voices::judge` today). `just check-door-placed` checks each of them is in bank 2 where it is linked.
  - Each ask's answer slot is empty (`None`) until bank 2 fills it: `Option<Turned>`, `Option<Result<(), Refusal>>` or `Option<Line>`. With the door closed it stays empty, so no seeded value can pass for an answer.
  - `part_voices::judge` calls `Shares::refusal` from one site and is built for speed, as `kit_groups`' checks are (4939da8c). A call repeated in bank-2 code is one the machine outliner may share from bank 1 (ADR 0175).
  - With the door closed, nothing is held, an edit lands unchecked, and both cells' focus band says the rule is off: `NO VOICE RULE · NEEDS UPDATE` (`view::RULE_OFF`, bank-1 text).
  - The words are byte statics read as text by a `const fn` whose `from_utf8` panics at compile time on bad bytes, so they link into bank 2 with their module. This is intentional, as `kit_groups`' words are.

## Alternatives considered
- **Static voice ownership, as stock does.** This splits 8 voices into fixed shares, so an idle Part's share is wasted. With six Parts on 8 voices that is too few for anyone. A floor that guards only against stealing keeps the pool shared.
- **A reserve that holds free voices back for its Part.** This is closer to stock. But it refuses another Part's note while voices sit free, which sounds like a bug. It also can't be bit-identical at 0 without a second free-voice path. Guarding against steals covers the reported case: the pad's held notes are what got stolen.
- **Shed never takes a guarded voice.** Then the load net could leave a block overrunning, which is worse for every Part, the guarded one included (ADR 0273's reset). Guarded voices go last instead.
- **Clamp a reserve to the cap silently, or scale reserves down at load.** That would change what the player saved without saying so. Refusing an edit on the cell and loading a project as saved matches ADR 0151 and 0280.
- **Part priority (keep one Part ringing whatever happens).** Filed as a separate follow-up in the issue. RESERVE covers the reported use.
- **Put MAX VOICES and RESERVE on PRT in place of BEND or OUT.** This moves cells players know. A sub-page under PRT keeps PRT as it was.

## Consequences
- A pad held on a Part with RESERVE 4 keeps its four voices while another Part plays dense runs. That Part steals only from the other Parts' voices (oldest first), or from its own.
- A capped Part can't grow past its cap. In multitimbral use this keeps one Part from taking the whole pool, as stock's per-timbre voices do, without fixing the split.
- Reserves summing to 8 can starve an unreserved Part when every voice is guarded: its note is refused. That is what the player asked for, and the reason is on the cell (`RESERVES 8/8`).
- The mixer's PRT map node gains one sub-page.
- Flash, measured on origin/0.4 1ff9f2a2 plus this branch, release builds. Bank 1: ORBIT +3828 B (890224 → 894052), default +3804, bank2-gate +3348, usb-config-writer +3852, bench and probe sets +3812 to +4396. The owner accepted the audio path's share on 2026-11-18: the allocator's cap, guard and stealable sets, the `Strike` hold, render's step 5 for a never-sounded voice, the card steal's argument, `voice_limit`'s types (`from_parts`; `seen` and `refusal` are inlined into bank 2), and `Voicing`'s decode at every MODE read. The rest is the two specs and their arms, VOICES' page, row word and nav, and the UI's out-of-line stubs (about 0.9 KB, one call per site behind the door). Bank 2: ORBIT +2688 B and default and bank2-gate +2816, for `ui::part_voices`. ORBIT's bank 1 then has 23 452 B under the 917 504 B SD-flash limit (`just firmware-orbit`'s stamped image: 894 084 B, 23 420 B under).
- AXI: `Voicing` keeps `PartParams` at 16 B, so the audio's snapshots don't grow. ORBIT has 44 B left over ADR 0087's 32 KiB margin (60 before this ADR).

## Supersedes in part
- [0015](0015-voice-steal-and-fx-returns.md) rule 1 ("steal … from any part"): a guarded Part's voices are not stolen by another Part's note, and a Part at its cap steals only its own voices, even with voices free.
- [0027](0027-shedding-fades-tails-first.md) "Order": guarded voices are shed last.
- [0273](0273-a-block-near-its-deadline-sheds-a-voice.md) "The response": the voice the net sheds is the one `shed_one` picks with guarded voices last. Its "a booked voice gone silent (a DRUM hit ended with its key held)" no longer occurs: a hit's slot frees when its voice finishes.
- [0183](0183-tape-streams-billed-by-the-reach-in-use.md) "Steals: released voices first, the oldest; then the quietest held": only among voices that aren't another Part's guarded ones (`Allocator::card_stealable`); with none that frees room, refused.
- [0280](0280-a-drum-kit-has-at-most-eight-voice-groups.md) "A pad with no live voice of its own … steals as rule 3 always has": at its Part's cap it steals within the Part.
- Extends [0151](0151-a-refused-kind-or-algorithm-is-shown-dimmed-with-its-reason.md)'s dimmed-with-reason refusal to MAX VOICES and RESERVE.
